What Water Damage Does to Electronics and When to Unplug: South Windsor, CT

Do not touch electrical equipment while standing in water. If the breaker panel is dry and reachable, shut off power to the wet area. Unplug wet electronics after the power is off, do not turn them on, and have a qualified electrician check wet outlets and wiring.

By the South Windsor Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 12, 2026

Safety first: power and water do not mix

Before you touch anything electrical

  • Never stand in water to reach a panel, outlet or cord.
  • If the panel is in a wet basement, do not go to it. Call the electric utility or 911.
  • If you smell gas or see sparks, leave the building and call 911 and the utility.
  • Only cut power at the panel if the floor around it is dry.
  • Have an electrician inspect wet outlets, panels and wiring before power is restored.

What water does to electronics

Water conducts electricity and corrodes metal contacts. A device that works after drying may fail weeks later from corrosion. Clean water is less damaging than flood or sewage water, which carries minerals and contaminants that leave residue.

Plugged-in devices are the biggest hazard because the power can energize water. Once power is off at the breaker, it is safe to unplug. Keep devices off until they are dry and checked.

Flood or sewage water means discard soaked electronics unless a professional advises otherwise.

Appliances and HVAC equipment

Refrigerators, washers and furnaces that sat in water often need service before they run again. A furnace or heat pump that was flooded should not be started. Water heaters and gas appliances need a licensed professional to check controls and gas valves.

If a basement flooded and the furnace sat in water, shut it off and call a heating contractor. Do not relight a pilot yourself.

South Windsor note: outages and flooding

The Capitol Region hazard mitigation plan records power outages of four to five days after the August 2020 storm in South Windsor. Long outages push people toward generators and extension cords, and wet conditions make those risky.

Never run a generator indoors or in an attached garage. Keep generators outside and away from windows, and keep cords out of standing water. For any downed line, stay away and call the utility.

After the water is gone

  1. Let everything dry fully before reconnecting.
  2. Have an electrician inspect the panel, outlets and wiring in the wet area.
  3. Have appliances tested by a technician before use.
  4. List damaged electronics with model and price for your claim.
  5. Photograph labels and serial numbers before you discard anything.
  6. Ask your insurer about electronics coverage.

The sooner the structure dries, the lower the corrosion risk. See structural drying for how readings set the end point and water extraction for removing standing water. If the source was an appliance, see appliance leak water damage. The same advice applies in East Windsor and Hartford.

A calm order of operations when power and water meet

  1. Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, cords or appliances.
  2. Call the electric utility or 911 if you cannot safely reach the panel.
  3. If the panel is dry and reachable, turn off the breaker for the wet area.
  4. Unplug devices only after the power is off.
  5. Photograph the area and list the items.
  6. Wait for an electrician before turning power back on.

This order is meant to keep you safe. It is not a guide for repairs. Electrical work in a wet building belongs to a licensed electrician.

Backups, batteries and surge protectors

Lithium batteries in phones, laptops and power tools can be a fire risk when damaged by water. Do not charge a wet device. Store it in an open area away from flammable items until it can be checked.

Surge protectors and power strips that got wet should be thrown away. Hidden corrosion can cause shorts later. Replace them rather than drying and reusing them.

Document electronics for your claim

List each item with brand, model, approximate age and price. Take photos of labels and serial numbers. Keep receipts if you have them. Your insurer may ask for this list, and it is easier to build before the items are discarded.

When appliances may be worth saving

Large appliances such as refrigerators and washers are built to handle some moisture, but their controls and motors are sensitive. A technician can test them before they are used again.

Whether repair is worthwhile depends on age and cost. If the water was contaminated, treat the appliance as suspect and ask the technician about cleaning and replacement.

  • Photograph the model and serial plate.
  • Keep the appliance unplugged until it is checked.
  • Ask about warranty or insurance coverage.

Smoke detectors, alarms and low-voltage systems

Water can also affect smoke detectors, security systems and thermostats. A wet detector may fail or give false alarms. Have these checked after a flood.

Low-voltage wiring in a wet wall may corrode over time. Ask an electrician to look at these systems when they inspect the wet area. Do not rely on a device just because the indicator light is on.

Once the power hazard is cleared, crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard to dry the structure.

Electronics questions

Is it safe to unplug a wet appliance?

Only if you are not standing in water and the power to the circuit is off. If the floor is wet and the panel is nearby, do not go near it.

Can I dry a phone in rice?

Rice is not reliable. Power the phone off, remove the case, and let it dry with airflow. See a technician if it does not recover.

Do wet outlets need replacing?

An electrician should inspect any outlet that got wet. Do not use it until it is cleared.

Can I use a generator after a South Windsor outage?

Only outside, away from windows and doors, and with dry cords. Never run one in a garage or basement.

Does the 24/7 line handle electrical hazards?

For any electrical or gas hazard, call 911 and the utility first. Then call the line for water damage.
