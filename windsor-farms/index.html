Windsor Farms and the town center

The National Register lists the Windsor Farms Historic District, added in 1986, at more than 1,600 acres. It is roughly bounded by Strong Road, Route 5, Interstate 291 and the Connecticut River, and Main Street runs through it from I-291 in the south to Strong Road in the north.

It is South Windsor's historic town center. The listing names the 1845 First Congregational Church, an 1845 district schoolhouse and the 1926 Wood Memorial Library. The oldest house dates to 1694, though many houses have been replaced by later construction, so the housing is a mix of ages.

Agricultural fields line both sides of Main Street, and many are still in tobacco. As with any district boundary, treat these limits as a guide, not an official neighborhood line.

What the hazard plan says about Strong Road

Strong Road is one of the district's edges, and it appears in the Capitol Region hazard plan. The plan lists Strong Road east of Main Street among flood-prone locations and names a Strong Road culvert there as a culvert of concern.

That is a road and a culvert. The plan does not say homes in Windsor Farms flooded. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, so check the FEMA flood map for your address.

The same plan records trees down, blocked roads and four to five days of power outages in South Windsor after the August 2020 storm. Town-wide events like that are context, not proof about one street.

Water problems that start inside the house

A mix of old and newer houses means a mix of plumbing and appliances, and we do not assume which a home has. The calls that start inside are the same everywhere: a split supply line, a failed water heater tank, a dishwasher hose or a leaking ceiling.

Begin with the page that matches the source. See water heater leak cleanup or ceiling water damage repair, then structural drying for what happens after the water is out.

Before help arrives

  • Stay out of any basement or room where water touches outlets, cords or the panel.
  • Turn off the water at the main only if you can reach it dry.
  • Never use a generator indoors or in an attached garage during an outage.
  • Do not drive through standing water on a road such as Strong Road.
  • Take dated photos and list what was wet.
  • For a downed line, gas smell or sparks, call 911 and the utility first.

Storm water and standing water

After a heavy rain, water can arrive through a foundation wall, a window well or a failed sump pump. Removing it quickly limits what it soaks. See storm water damage cleanup and basement water damage cleanup for the process, and water extraction if there is standing water now.

Damp drywall and insulation can support mold growth, so do not close walls over wet material. Water that came from a river, a storm drain or a sewer line is treated as contaminated, which changes the gear and the materials that can be saved.

Insurance records

Connecticut Insurance Department guidance is to take photos and video first, save receipts and tell your insurer without delay. Flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners coverage, so ask your insurer about any separate flood policy. Photos and moisture readings help describe the work but do not guarantee payment.

Windsor Farms questions

Does Windsor Farms flood?

The hazard plan lists a Strong Road culvert and a flood-prone stretch of Strong Road, but it does not name Windsor Farms homes as flooded. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified, so check the FEMA map for your address.

Who do I call for a power outage during a storm?

Call your electric utility for outages and downed lines. Call 911 for fire, gas smells or danger. Call us for the water damage once everyone is safe.

Can I run a wet vacuum on a flooded basement?

A wet vacuum is fine for a small amount of clean water on a dry outlet. For deeper, dirty or contaminated water, or any water near electrical equipment, wait for a crew.

Will insurance pay for storm water damage?

It depends on how the water entered and on your policy. Flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies. Ask your insurer before you rely on any answer.
