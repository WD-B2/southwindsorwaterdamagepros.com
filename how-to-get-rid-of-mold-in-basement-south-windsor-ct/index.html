How to Get Rid of Mold in a Basement: A South Windsor, CT Homeowner Guide

Fix the moisture first, then clean small hard-surface spots with detergent and water, and remove porous materials that are moldy. Large or hidden growth calls for a professional. Mold returns if the basement stays damp.

(844) 950-6900

By the South Windsor Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 12, 2026

Step one: find the moisture

Mold needs water. Without fixing the source, cleaning only buys time. Look for a leaking pipe, a failed sump pump, seepage through a wall, condensation on cold pipes or a dryer vent that exhausts indoors.

A moisture meter on walls and floors, and a hygrometer in the air, help find the problem. Sustained high humidity gives mold a reason to grow.

What you can handle and what you should not

Safe cleaning basics

  1. Stop the moisture source and dry the area.
  2. Wear gloves, eye protection and an N95 or better respirator.
  3. Close the area off and avoid running fans that spread dust through the house.
  4. Wipe hard surfaces with detergent and water and dry them completely.
  5. Bag porous materials that are moldy and take them out.
  6. Keep humidity under control with a dehumidifier.

Do not mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners. Bleach does not reach inside porous materials. Never scrape dry mold or blow it around with a fan.

When the job needs a professional

  • Growth covers a large area or goes inside walls.
  • The mold came from sewage or flood water.
  • Someone in the home has a medical concern. Ask a physician.
  • The smell is strong and the source is unknown.
  • A lender, buyer or insurer needs documentation.

A professional job follows containment, removal and verification and includes fixing the moisture. For details, see the mold remediation page.

South Windsor note: basements, brooks and sump pumps

The Capitol Region hazard mitigation plan names the Podunk River and Averys, Bancroft, Dry, Newberry and Waples Brooks among the town's watercourses, and heavy rain raises groundwater near low ground. A basement near one of them may see seasonal dampness.

The same plan records outages of four to five days after the August 2020 storm. A sump pump stops without power, so a battery backup is worth considering. The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection says residential mold remediation work falls under a home improvement contractor registration with NORMI or IICRC certification. Ask any company about its credentials.

Keeping the basement dry afterward

  • Point downspouts away from the foundation.
  • Keep soil sloped away from the house.
  • Run a dehumidifier and empty it or drain it.
  • Insulate cold water pipes to stop condensation.
  • Store items on shelves and not on the floor.
  • Check the sump pump twice a year.

No one can promise mold will not return, because mold depends on moisture. If water returns, see basement water damage cleanup. Homes in Broad Brook and East Windsor follow the same rules.

A basement humidity routine that works

Mold needs moisture, food and time. Basements offer plenty of the first two. Keeping indoor humidity moderate is the most reliable way to stop growth from returning after a cleanup.

A hygrometer costs little and shows the humidity at a glance. Many guides suggest keeping indoor humidity below about 60 percent, and lower is better in damp seasons. Run a dehumidifier and empty it on schedule.

  1. Place a hygrometer in the basement and check it weekly.
  2. Run a dehumidifier with a drain hose if you can.
  3. Keep boxes off the floor and a few inches from walls.
  4. Clean the dehumidifier filter each month.
  5. Check for new stains after heavy rain.
  6. Fix any plumbing drip the same week.

Why bleach is not the answer

Bleach can lighten stains on hard surfaces, but it does not reach the roots of mold in porous materials like drywall, wood and fabric. It also leaves moisture behind. Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, because the fumes are dangerous.

Dish soap and water on a hard surface, followed by drying, handles small areas safely. Porous materials with growth are usually removed and replaced.

Drainage outside the house matters too

Gutters, downspouts and the soil slope decide how much water reaches the foundation. Downspouts should discharge several feet from the house. Soil should slope away from the walls. Cracks in the foundation should be sealed by a qualified contractor.

These fixes reduce the water that the basement has to manage, which makes every indoor step more effective.

What to throw away and what to keep

Porous items with visible growth, such as carpet, cardboard and upholstered furniture, are usually discarded. Hard items, such as plastic bins, metal shelves and glass, can be cleaned and dried.

Bag moldy items before you carry them through the house, so spores are not spread. Wear a mask and gloves while you handle them.

  • Discard wet carpet and pad.
  • Discard stained ceiling tiles.
  • Clean plastic and metal with detergent.
  • Dry wood fully and check it for soft spots.

Signs the moisture problem is not solved

  • New spots appear within weeks of cleaning.
  • A damp or musty smell returns after rain.
  • White mineral deposits show on masonry walls.
  • Paint or finish bubbles on the lower wall.
  • The dehumidifier tank fills faster than expected.

Any of these means water is still arriving. Cleaning mold without fixing the source only buys time. Follow the damp trail to its origin, which may be a gutter, a crack, a pipe or a window well, and fix that first.

Basement mold questions

Can I just paint over basement mold?

No. Paint does not stop mold when moisture remains, and it can peel. Clean or remove the growth and fix the water source first.

Is bleach the best way to kill mold?

Bleach works on hard surfaces only and can be dangerous when mixed. Detergent and water with drying is often enough for small spots.

Does a dehumidifier get rid of basement mold?

It does not remove mold that is already growing, but it lowers the humidity that feeds it. Use it after cleaning.

What credentials should a mold company in Connecticut have?

The Department of Consumer Protection says residential mold work falls under a home improvement contractor registration and that NORMI or IICRC certification is required. Ask the company to show it.
