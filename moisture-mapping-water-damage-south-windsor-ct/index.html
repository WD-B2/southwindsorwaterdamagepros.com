Moisture Mapping After Water Damage: A South Windsor, CT Homeowner Guide

A moisture map is a record of moisture readings taken at fixed points in every wet room. It shows where water went, how wet each material is and how fast it is drying. Without one, no one can tell when a house is really dry.

(844) 950-6900

By the South Windsor Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 12, 2026

What a moisture map shows

After water damage, the surface can look dry while the wood and drywall behind it stay wet. A moisture map puts numbers on that hidden water. The crew reads moisture content in floors, walls and framing and records the readings by location.

The first set of readings shows the wet area. Later sets show whether it is shrinking. When the numbers in the wet area match numbers in an unaffected area of the same material, the structure is dry.

The tools behind the readings

A thermal camera shows where to look, and a moisture meter confirms the reading.
ToolWhat it measuresBest for
Pin moisture meterMoisture content in wood and drywall through two pinsDirect readings in framing and subfloor
Pinless meterMoisture below the surface using a sensor padQuick scans of walls and floors
Thermal imaging cameraSurface temperature differencesFinding wet patches behind drywall
HygrometerAir temperature and humidityTracking room conditions during drying
Concrete moisture testMoisture in a slabChecking before new flooring

How readings are taken

  1. The crew picks a reference point in an unaffected room of the same material.
  2. Readings are taken at marked points on walls, floors and trim in each wet room.
  3. The points are recorded on a sketch or a form with the date.
  4. Readings repeat each visit at the same points.
  5. Equipment moves as areas dry, and the readings guide the move.

Why the map matters for your claim and your health of the structure

An adjuster can see from the readings that drying was needed and when it ended. That helps explain why equipment ran for several days.

Readings also protect the building. Rebuilding over damp framing traps moisture and invites mold. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes this drying approach, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow it.

Typical dry targets and what they mean

Dry targets are set by comparing to a reference reading, not by a single number for all homes. Drywall in a dry room reads very low. Wood framing in an occupied Connecticut house sits at a moisture content that depends on the season and the heating.

That is why a reference reading in the same house matters. The crew writes down the dry standard on day one and works toward it.

South Windsor note: humidity and the seasons

Connecticut summers are humid and winters are dry indoors when the heat is on. Wood and drywall move with those seasons, which is another reason to compare against a reference reading in the same house and not a number from a chart.

The South Windsor Building Department can tell you whether repairs need a permit, so ask before opening and closing walls. The office number is (860) 644-2511, extension 2330.

Questions to ask about moisture mapping

  • Will you take a reference reading in an unaffected room?
  • Will I get the readings in writing each visit?
  • How will you decide when equipment can come out?
  • Are walls opened or drilled to check cavities?
  • Who decides when the structure is dry?

A worked example of a moisture map

Picture a first floor where a dishwasher hose failed. The crew starts in a dry room and records a baseline. Then they read the kitchen floor, the toe-kick of the cabinets and the wall behind the dishwasher at set heights.

The readings show a wet zone that is wider than the visible stain. The map marks it with dated points. Fans and dehumidifiers go in, and the same points are read again each visit.

The pattern is typical, not a promise. Real jobs vary by material and weather.
VisitWhat is checkedWhat the numbers show
Day 1Baseline plus every wet areaWide wet zone, higher readings near the source
Day 2Same pointsReadings falling at the surface, slower in thick materials
Day 3Same points plus edgesEdges near normal, a few deep spots still high
FinalAll points against the baselineReadings back in line with the dry room

Common mistakes with meters

  • Reading only where the surface looks wet.
  • Skipping a dry baseline for comparison.
  • Using the wrong setting for the material.
  • Trusting one reading on one day.
  • Ignoring heat or foil-backed insulation that can distort a reading.

A meter is a tool, not a verdict. Thermal imaging can show cool wet areas, but it does not measure moisture on its own. Crews confirm what a camera shows with a contact reading. Ask to see the map and the numbers, not just hear that things are dry.

How to read a moisture report as a homeowner

A good report lists each reading location, the material, the number and the date. It names the dry reference value. It notes where readings fell and where they did not.

If the report is only a sentence, ask for more. You are entitled to understand what the crew measured, and your insurer may ask for the same data.

Moisture maps and different building materials

Drywall, wood, concrete and plaster hold water differently. A wall may read dry on the surface while the stud behind it stays wet. Concrete can take a long time to release moisture, and readings depend on the tool used.

That is why crews record the material with each reading. A number alone means little. A number with a material and a dry reference tells you whether to stop or keep drying.

Ask which tool was used for which material, and why.

What to do before the readings are taken

  1. Leave wet materials in place so the crew can read them.
  2. Do not run a heater to dry the area first.
  3. Mark the stained areas with painter's tape.
  4. Write down when the leak started and when it stopped.
  5. Photograph each wet area from the same spot.

Moisture mapping questions

Can I buy a moisture meter and check myself?

You can, and a cheap meter helps in small cases. Professional meters and thermal cameras are better at finding hidden water, and readings need interpretation.

How many days of readings are normal?

Many jobs take three to five days of monitoring, but timing depends on materials and the amount of water.

Does a dry surface mean the wall is dry?

No. Water can stay inside the wall cavity and in the framing while the surface looks fine. A meter or camera shows it.

Does a Connecticut winter change drying?

Yes. Heated indoor air is dry and helps drying, while humid summer air slows it. The crew adjusts equipment to the season.

Does South Windsor require a permit to open walls?

It depends on the work. Call the Building Department at (860) 644-2511, extension 2330, before rebuilding.
