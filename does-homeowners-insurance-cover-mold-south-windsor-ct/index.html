Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Mold? A South Windsor, CT Homeowner Guide

It depends on the cause. Many policies cover mold that results from a covered sudden water loss, often with limits. They commonly exclude mold from long-term leaks or neglect. Read the mold and fungi section of your policy and ask your insurer.

(844) 950-6900

By the South Windsor Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 12, 2026

Why mold coverage is tricky

Mold is a result, not a cause of loss. Insurers look at what caused the moisture. If a covered peril, such as a sudden pipe burst, led to mold, the policy may pay for the mold as part of that loss. If the moisture came from a slow leak or humidity, the claim may be denied.

Many policies include a mold or fungi limit, a sublimit or an exclusion. The wording differs between insurers, so a friend's claim tells you little about yours.

These are patterns, not promises. Your policy wording governs.

What to read in your policy

  • The section on mold, fungi or wet rot.
  • Any limit on mold remediation or testing.
  • The wording on continuous or repeated seepage and leakage.
  • Whether you added a water backup endorsement.
  • What your policy says about your duty to prevent further damage.

Ask your agent to point to the exact language. Keep a copy of the policy with your emergency papers.

How quick drying changes a mold claim

Insurers often ask whether you acted promptly. Drying a wet structure within a day or two limits mold growth and shows you took reasonable steps. Photographs, moisture readings and receipts record that.

The Connecticut Insurance Department says it is generally your obligation to prevent further damage. Quick extraction and drying are part of that.

South Windsor note: guidance from state sources

The Connecticut Insurance Department advises documenting damage with photos and videos before cleanup, keeping receipts and contacting your insurer promptly. It also says not to feel rushed to settle. Its Consumer Affairs Division can be reached at 860-297-3900.

The Department of Consumer Protection says mold remediation under a home improvement contractor registration covers residential work and requires NORMI or IICRC certification. Your insurer may ask for documentation of who did the work and what they did.

Steps if you find mold

  1. Find and stop the moisture source.
  2. Photograph the growth and the area before disturbing it.
  3. Notify your insurer and ask how mold is handled in your policy.
  4. Get a written scope for removal and the price before work starts.
  5. Dry the structure and keep daily readings.
  6. Keep receipts and the verification report.

Common mold wording and what it may mean

Policies use different words for mold. Some exclude it unless it results from a covered water loss. Some set a low limit for mold cleanup. Some treat fungi, wet rot and dry rot together.

Read the exclusions page and any endorsements. If you cannot tell what a clause means, ask your insurer to explain it in writing.

How the cause of the water changes the answer

Mold that grows after a sudden covered event, such as a burst pipe, may be handled differently from mold that grows from a leak that went unnoticed for months. This is why the cause and the timeline matter.

Document when you found the water and what you did next. Fast drying records show you acted to prevent mold, which supports your position.

Mold and a home sale or a rental

If you are selling, a mold finding may affect the sale even if insurance does not pay for it. If you rent out the property, check what your landlord policy says. Ask a lawyer or a state agency if you have questions about disclosure rules.

What to do before you hire a mold company

Call your insurer before major mold work starts, unless health or safety is at risk. Ask whether a sub-limit applies and whether they want to inspect first. Take photos of the growth and the damp area, and keep any samples or reports.

Then get a written scope that separates the water repair from the mold work. This helps if the insurer pays for one part and not the other.

  • Keep the invoice for each stage in a separate line.
  • Ask the company to note the moisture source it found.
  • Keep the clearance report if one is done.
  • Record dates for every call with the insurer.

Do not let a coverage question delay the fix

Mold spreads when materials stay wet. If you are waiting on an answer, take reasonable steps to dry the area and stop the source. Keep receipts. Your policy may require you to prevent further damage, and records of what you did can support your case later.

If the claim is denied, ask for the reason in writing. The Connecticut Insurance Department Consumer Affairs line, 860-297-3900, takes questions about insurance.

Mold insurance questions

Does homeowners insurance cover mold from a burst pipe?

It may, often with a limit, since the mold followed a sudden covered loss. Read your mold section and ask your insurer.

Will insurance pay for mold from a leak I did not notice?

Often not if the leak was gradual or long-term. Some policies cover hidden leaks that were sudden, so the wording matters.

Does flood insurance cover mold?

It depends on the policy. Ask your flood insurer how it treats mold that results from a covered flood.

Should I remove mold before the adjuster visits?

Photograph first. Ask your insurer before you discard materials. Stopping the water and starting drying is usually expected.

Does the 24/7 line decide coverage?

No. Coverage decisions belong to your insurer. The crew provides readings, photos and a written scope.
