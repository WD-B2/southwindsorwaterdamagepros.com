Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in South Windsor, CT

Wood absorbs water and moves. Boards cup, crown or buckle days after a leak, so early drying makes the difference between saving a floor and replacing it. Call the 24/7 line to start drying.

(844) 950-6900

Hardwood floors are wood, and wood takes on water and swells. The swelling pushes boards against each other, so the edges rise into a cup or the whole floor lifts. Surface stains and dark gray patches are signs that water has stayed on the finish or soaked into the grain.

If you see standing water on a hardwood floor, soak it up and call the 24/7 line. Do not wait for the boards to move. Same-day response is available when crews are open, and drying started in the first day or two gives the best chance of saving the floor.

What water does to wood floors

Engineered wood and laminate behave differently from solid hardwood. Laminate swells permanently once water gets into the core.

Drying hardwood: the steps

The crew extracts surface water and checks moisture content in the boards and the subfloor. On many floors, specialty drying mats pull moisture upward through the wood while air movers push dry air across the surface. A dehumidifier keeps room humidity low enough to carry moisture away.

Drying is slow and must not be rushed, since wood that dries too fast cracks. The crew checks readings every day. The floor is dry when board moisture matches a reference reading for a dry floor in the same room.

Sanding, refinishing or replacing

After drying, cupped boards often settle and can be sanded flat. A sand and refinish is much cheaper than replacement. Boards that buckled or split are replaced, and new boards are blended into the old floor. Stain matching can be the hardest part, particularly on older floors.

Do not sand until the floor is fully dry. Sanding wet wood leaves a floor that cups again. Wait for the written moisture readings.

Where the water often comes from

Hardwood most often gets wet from kitchen and bath leaks, appliance failures, plant pots, pet accidents, roof leaks and basement humidity. A floor over a damp crawl space can cup with no visible water at all. See crawl space water damage for that case.

For a leak that started in the kitchen, see kitchen water damage restoration. If water soaked the walls too, wall and drywall water damage repair covers the baseboards.

What to do before the boards start to cup

  1. Soak up standing water right away with towels.
  2. Lift rugs, mats and furniture off the wet boards.
  3. Do not seal or refinish the floor while it is wet.
  4. Open windows and run fans, not heaters.
  5. Do not use a household steam mop on a wet floor.

Wood absorbs water slowly, so it can look fine while the boards are still wet. Crews classify water and plan drying under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration.

Hardwood water damage questions

Can a cupped hardwood floor be saved?

Often yes, if it is dried promptly and slowly. Many floors flatten as they dry and are then sanded. Severe buckling usually needs board replacement.

How long should I wait before sanding?

Until the floor is fully dry, which is confirmed by moisture readings and not by appearance. Sanding too soon leaves a floor that cups again.

What about laminate flooring?

Laminate has a fiberboard core that swells when wet and does not return to shape, so wet laminate is usually replaced.

Does the 24/7 line help with a leak that happened days ago?

Yes. The line is open 24/7 and an older leak can still be dried, though earlier is better.
