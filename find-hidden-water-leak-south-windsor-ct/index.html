How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Home: A South Windsor, CT Guide

Turn off every fixture and watch the water meter. If it moves, water is escaping. Then check for stains, warm floor spots, musty smells and the sound of running water. A plumber with detection tools can pinpoint a leak behind a wall or under a slab.

By the South Windsor Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 12, 2026

Start with the water meter

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and the ice maker.
  2. Find the meter, usually near where the line enters the house or in a box by the street.
  3. Look at the leak indicator, a small dial or triangle that spins when water flows.
  4. Note the reading, wait 20 to 30 minutes with no water use and read it again.
  5. If the number changed, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and a fixture.

To narrow it down, shut off the main valve at the house and watch the meter. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside.

The toilet dye test

A toilet that runs quietly can waste many gallons. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

A flapper is cheap to replace. Do this before you assume a larger leak.

Checking outside and under the house

  • Look at the ground near the meter and the foundation for wet spots in dry weather.
  • Check outdoor spigots and irrigation for drips.
  • Inspect the crawl space or basement ceiling for dripping pipes.
  • Look at the water heater and its connections.
  • Check under sinks and behind appliances.

South Windsor note: who owns which pipe

Connecticut Water has replaced aging mains in South Windsor. In one project on High Street, Hillside Drive, Steep Road, Aroda Drive and Greenfield Drive, it replaced pipe the utility described as about 62 years old. The utility owns the main under the street, and the line from the shutoff into your house is typically yours.

If your meter keeps moving after the main shutoff is closed, call your water utility and a plumber. The Town's Water Pollution Control Authority handles the sewer side, not the water supply. Ask your utility if you are unsure who serves your street.

When to call a plumber and when to call restoration

A plumber finds and repairs the leak. A restoration crew dries what the water soaked. If you find wet drywall, a swollen baseboard or a soft floor, call both.

The sooner the leak is stopped, the less material is lost. For leaks inside walls, see wall and drywall water damage repair. For a leak in the floor slab, see slab leak water damage. A visible split pipe is covered in burst pipe water damage.

Leak detection devices worth considering

  • Flow sensors that shut off water when usage looks abnormal.
  • Small floor sensors under sinks, water heaters and washers.
  • Smart water meters or a monthly meter log.
  • Alarm sensors near the sump pit.

These do not stop a leak by themselves, but they can alert you. Homes in East Windsor and Hartford use the same tools.

A one-hour leak hunt you can do yourself

  1. Turn off all taps and appliances that use water.
  2. Write down the water meter reading, then wait an hour without using water.
  3. Read it again. Movement means water is flowing somewhere.
  4. Close the main valve and read the meter again. If it still moves, the leak is before the valve.
  5. Check toilet tanks with a few drops of food coloring.
  6. Look under every sink and behind the washer and refrigerator.
  7. Check the water heater for drips or rust at the base.

Take photos of what you find. They help the plumber and your insurer. If the meter moves with everything off, you likely have a hidden leak.

Clues that a leak is inside a wall or under a floor

  • A water bill that rises with no change in use.
  • Warm spots on a floor, which can point to a hot water line.
  • Hissing or running sounds when everything is off.
  • Peeling paint, bubbling wallpaper or soft baseboards.
  • A musty smell in a closed room.
  • Cracks or stains that grow over weeks.

Why speed matters for hidden leaks

A hidden leak keeps wetting the same materials. Wood swells, drywall softens and mold can start. The longer it runs, the more of the building it reaches.

When you find signs of a leak, repair the source first. Then have the wet area dried and checked with a meter, so the repair is not hiding damp materials.

What to tell the plumber

Share what you found: the meter test result, the rooms with signs, the age of the plumbing and when you first noticed the problem. Photos of stains help. The more you can describe, the less time is spent searching.

Seasonal leak risks to keep in mind

Cold snaps can split pipes in unheated spaces, and thaws reveal the damage. Summer humidity can make condensation on cold pipes look like a leak. Heavy rain can push groundwater into a basement and mimic a plumbing problem.

Match the season to the symptom. A wet wall in January points toward a pipe. A wet floor after a storm points toward drainage. Checking the meter test settles the question.

Restoration crews rely on the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard to plan drying once a leak is stopped.

Hidden leak questions

Can a slab leak happen in a Connecticut home?

Yes where there is a slab floor, such as an addition or a garage. The meter test and warm floor spots are clues. A plumber confirms the location.

How much water does a small leak waste?

It varies. A running toilet can waste many gallons a day. Your meter shows what is really being lost.

Who owns the pipe from the street to my house in South Windsor?

The utility owns the main under the street, and the line from the shutoff to your house is typically yours. Ask your water utility to confirm for your property.

Does insurance pay to find a leak?

Some policies cover the cost of finding a leak when there is covered damage, and others do not. Ask your insurer before you hire.

Do I need to dry the area after the leak is fixed?

Yes. Water that soaked drywall, framing or flooring stays unless dried. The 24/7 line can arrange drying.
