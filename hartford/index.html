If water is still spreading: before the crew arrives

1

Stop the source

Close the main water valve if a pipe or appliance is leaking. In an apartment, tell the landlord or manager so the building supply can be isolated.

2

Protect power

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable. Call the utility for any spark or smell of gas.

3

Lift valuables

Raise small items, papers and electronics off the floor. Leave heavy furniture where it stands.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Give the full address and building access details so the crew can find you.

Rivers, dikes and the buried Park River

Hartford drains to the Connecticut River on the east and the Park River on the west. The Park River was nearly completely enclosed and buried by flood control projects in the 1940s, and the Army Corps of Engineers built dikes along the Connecticut River after floods in 1936 and 1938. The regional plan says the city has six flood control pump stations.

The plan also describes Hartford's sewer system as old and primarily combined, which means stormwater and sewage share pipes. Heavy rain can strain that system, so a basement backup is a real possibility in a storm. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about backup coverage.

What to do when water enters an older building

  1. Shut off the water at the main or ask the building manager to do it.
  2. Keep away from wet electrical panels and outlets.
  3. Do not use a basement drain or toilet during a sewer backup.
  4. Move belongings to a higher shelf or floor.
  5. Tell your landlord or property manager in writing.
