Emergency Ceiling Water Damage Repair in South Windsor, CT

A stained or sagging ceiling means water is trapped above it. Keep people out from under the bulge, then call the 24/7 line. This page also covers roof leaks that reach the ceiling.

(844) 950-6900

A ceiling stain is a symptom, not the problem. The problem is water above the drywall, whether from a roof leak, an upstairs bathroom, a supply line or an air handler in the attic. The longer it stays, the heavier the drywall gets and the closer it comes to dropping.

If the ceiling is bulging or dripping, keep people and pets away, put a bucket under the drip and poke a small relief hole only if you can do it safely at arm's length. Then call the 24/7 line. Same-day response is available when crews are open.

Ceiling bulging: what to do right now

  • Move furniture and electronics away from the area.
  • Turn off any ceiling fixture or fan on the wet section at the wall switch. Do not touch them.
  • Shut off the main water valve if the source could be an upstairs pipe.
  • Photograph the stain before it grows, and mark its edge with a pencil to track spread.
  • Call (844) 950-6900.

Finding the source above the ceiling

Water travels. A stain in a living room may start at a bathroom two rooms away, because the water follows joists and wires. The crew uses a moisture meter and a thermal camera to trace the wet area back to the source. Opening a small inspection hole is better than guessing.

If the source is plumbing, the plumber fixes it and restoration dries the cavity. If the source is the roof, a roofer fixes it. Many homeowners need both trades for the same loss.

Roof leaks that reach the ceiling

A roof leak is usually a ceiling problem first. Missing shingles, flashing around chimneys and vents, and clogged gutters let water into the attic and down into insulation. Ice dams in winter push melting snow back up under shingles.

The Capitol Region hazard mitigation plan names winter storms as a recurring hazard for South Windsor and records a large 2015 storm with more than $8 million in damage according to Town officials. Heavy snow sitting on a roof makes ice dams and attic leaks more likely when it melts.

A temporary tarp and a roofer visit stop the water. Restoration removes wet insulation, dries the ceiling and framing, and replaces damaged drywall. For storm damage across the whole house, see storm water damage cleanup.

Repair, not just paint

Paint over a stain hides it and traps moisture. Proper repair means dry readings first, then removing damaged drywall, treating the framing if needed, patching and finishing. Textured ceilings take extra work to match.

If the ceiling has been wet for more than a day or two, check for mold on the back of the drywall. That is covered under mold remediation, which follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

Cost factors

  • Size of the wet area and whether the ceiling is flat or textured.
  • Insulation that is wet and has to be removed.
  • Whether the source is plumbing or the roof.
  • Drying equipment time.
  • Paint, texture and finishing.

Water from a sudden roof or plumbing event may be covered by your policy, while long-term seepage often is not. Ask your insurer and document the damage. Call (844) 950-6900 for scope and price before work starts.

Ceiling water damage questions

Do you handle ceiling water emergencies at night or on weekends in South Windsor?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. A ceiling leak is often found at night when the house goes quiet. Call, describe the stain and a crew is arranged for the next available time. Arrival time is not guaranteed.

Is a sagging ceiling dangerous?

Yes. Wet drywall can drop suddenly, and the weight of trapped water can be significant. Keep people away and call right away.

Do I need to fix the roof first?

If the roof is the source, a temporary repair must stop the water before drying can finish. Otherwise the ceiling gets wet again. Restoration and roofing are separate trades, and both are usually needed.

How long does ceiling repair take?

Drying often takes several days, followed by patching, texture and paint. Timing depends on the size of the wet area and whether insulation and framing are involved.
