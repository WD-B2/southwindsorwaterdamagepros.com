What to Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage: South Windsor, CT

The deciding factor is the water category. Items soaked by clean water are often saved if dried quickly. Porous items soaked by sewage or flood water are usually discarded. Photograph everything before you throw it out, and keep damaged items until your adjuster allows disposal when you safely can.

Start with the water category

Guidance follows the water categories in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Photograph before you discard

The Connecticut Insurance Department says to take photos and videos of damage to your home and belongings before moving or discarding anything. It also says to keep damaged items when possible until your insurer can inspect them.

If something is a hazard and must go, photograph it, note what it is and what it was worth. A simple list with item, age and price supports a claim.

Items that almost always go

  • Food, drinks and medicines exposed to flood or sewage water.
  • Mattresses, pillows and stuffed toys exposed to contaminated water.
  • Carpet pad.
  • Particleboard furniture swollen by water.
  • Cosmetics and open toiletries touched by flood water.
  • Anything that smells of sewage and cannot be washed hot.

Items worth trying to save

Photographs, important papers, wooden furniture, tools and hard goods are often worth saving. Rinse mud off hard goods quickly. Lay papers flat or freeze them to buy time. A freezer stops mold growth on paper until you can get help drying.

Electronics are a special case. Unplug them and do not turn them on after they got wet. See the electronics post for the specifics, and ask a repair professional.

South Windsor note: the flood caution

The Connecticut Insurance Department notes that flood damage is generally not covered by a standard homeowners policy and that vehicle flood damage may be covered under comprehensive auto insurance. Contents damaged by flood often are not covered unless you have flood coverage for contents.

The Capitol Region hazard mitigation plan names the Connecticut, Scantic and Hockanum Rivers among South Windsor's watercourses. If your belongings were soaked by flood water, treat them as contaminated and photograph them before disposal.

How to document and remove safely

  1. Wear gloves and boots, and use a mask for moldy items.
  2. Photograph each item and write it on a list.
  3. Bag soft goods and set them outside the work area.
  4. Move salvageable items to a dry room.
  5. Keep a few representative samples of discarded carpet or pad if the adjuster asks.
  6. Keep receipts for storage and disposal.

A restoration crew can pack out and clean contents when needed. For wet carpet, see carpet water damage restoration. For contamination, see sewage backup cleanup. The same rules apply in East Windsor and Hartford.

Room by room: what to look at

Kitchen

Discard food that touched flood or sewage water, including items in packaging that cannot be sealed. Clean and disinfect hard dishes and metal pots. Cabinets made of particleboard often swell and fail, while solid wood may dry.

Living room and bedrooms

Upholstered furniture and mattresses soaked by clean water may be dried and cleaned if you act fast. If the water was gray or contaminated, plan to replace them. Photograph tags and labels before disposal.

Basement and garage

Cardboard boxes, paper goods and fabric often hold water and mold. Plastic bins can be wiped and dried. Tools and metal items can be rinsed, dried and oiled to prevent rust.

Salvage and cleaning basics

  • Wash hard items with detergent and warm water, then disinfect when the water was contaminated.
  • Dry everything fully before storing it.
  • Use gloves and boots for any item that touched flood or sewage water.
  • Wash fabrics hot when the label allows it.
  • Send high-value items to a specialist, not the curb.

If you are unsure, keep the item in a dry area, photograph it and ask your insurer or a restoration professional before you throw it out.

Using a simple tag system

When you sort belongings, use three zones. Put items to keep in one area, items to clean in another and items to discard in a third. Label each zone and photograph it. This keeps you from mixing clean goods with contaminated ones.

Write a list as you go. Include the item, its age, an estimated price and a note on its condition. Your insurer may ask for the list, and it helps you remember what you owned.

Disposal and storage

Check town rules for bulky item pickup and disposal. Ask whether mattresses or electronics have special handling. If your belongings need to be stored while the structure dries, ask your restoration company about pack-out and storage, and keep the receipts.

Keep a few photos of the pile before it leaves, so the record shows what was removed and when.

Children, pets and sensitive items

Soft toys, pet bedding and baby items soaked by contaminated water should be discarded. Hard plastic toys can be washed and disinfected. Pet bowls and crates can be cleaned thoroughly.

Medicines, cosmetics and baby formula that touched flood or sewage water should go. If you are unsure about a specific item, ask a pharmacist or your doctor.

Keep children and pets out of the work area until it has been cleaned and dried.

Contents questions

Can I keep a couch that got wet?

Sometimes. A couch soaked by clean water and dried quickly may be saved. Upholstery soaked by sewage or flood water is usually discarded.

Should I throw out food from a wet refrigerator?

Food that touched flood or sewage water should go. Food in sealed cans may be cleaned and kept if the can is not damaged, though check current guidance.

Will my insurer pay for ruined belongings?

It depends on the cause and your policy. Photograph and list everything and ask your insurer.

Can I save wet photographs?

Often yes. Separate them gently, rinse mud with clean water and dry them flat, or freeze them until you can get help.

Is flood damage to belongings covered in South Windsor?

A standard homeowners policy generally excludes flood. Check whether you have flood coverage for contents.
