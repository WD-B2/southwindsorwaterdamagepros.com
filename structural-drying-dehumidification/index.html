Structural Drying and Dehumidification in South Windsor, CT

Structural drying removes moisture from framing, subfloor, walls and flooring after the standing water is gone. It uses air movers, dehumidifiers and moisture readings. Call the 24/7 line to start.

(844) 950-6900

After a leak or flood, the visible water is only part of the problem. Water soaks into wood, drywall, insulation and concrete, and it stays there until it is dried out. Structural drying is the process of removing that moisture on a measured schedule.

This is the step many people call water mitigation. It follows extraction and comes before repair. Call the 24/7 line if your walls feel cool and damp, your floor feels soft or a musty smell lingers. Same-day response is available when crews are open.

Walls still feel damp and the house smells musty?

People rarely search for structural drying by name. They search for things like how to dry out a house after a leak, wet drywall will not dry, musty smell after flood or how long does it take for walls to dry. Each of those is the same problem. Moisture is trapped inside the building materials and needs equipment to leave.

The trade term is structural drying and dehumidification, and it means controlled air movement and moisture removal until every material reads dry. Fans alone do not do this, because moist air has to be taken out of the room, not just moved around.

How structural drying works

  1. Moisture mapping. A moisture meter and a thermal camera find wet areas and mark them.
  2. Equipment setup. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, and dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air.
  3. Monitoring. The crew records temperature, humidity and material readings each visit.
  4. Adjusting. Equipment moves as areas dry, and wall cavities may be opened or vented.
  5. Final readings. The job ends when the materials match a dry reference.
The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides drying goals and documentation. Ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Moisture mapping water damage: what it tells you

A moisture map is a record of readings in each wet room, taken at fixed points. It shows where water is, how wet it is and how fast it is drying. It is also the evidence an insurance adjuster uses to see that drying was needed.

Good moisture mapping tells you when to stop, which prevents two common failures. One is ending drying too early, which leads to mold. The other is running equipment for days after materials are already dry.

How long it takes

Many jobs take three to five days, but timing depends on the materials, the amount of water and the weather. Dense materials like hardwood and plaster take longer. Wet insulation and trapped cavities slow drying down.

Summer humidity in Connecticut slows evaporation, while a heated house in winter dries faster. The crew adjusts equipment to the conditions and reports daily progress.

Cost factors

  • Number of rooms and square feet to dry.
  • Equipment count and days on site.
  • Materials, such as hardwood or plaster, that dry slowly.
  • Whether walls are opened.
  • Monitoring visits.

Structural drying questions

How do I know when a house is dry?

A moisture meter compares each material to a dry reference. The house is dry when wet areas read the same as unaffected areas. Feel and smell are not reliable.

Why use a dehumidifier and not just fans?

Fans move air but do not remove moisture from the room. A dehumidifier pulls the moisture out so the air can keep absorbing more from the wet materials.

Can I dry a house myself?

For a small, clean spill on a hard floor, fans and towels may work. For wet walls, carpet pad or framing, a meter and the right equipment are the only way to be sure.

What is water mitigation?

It means stopping the damage from getting worse, by removing water and drying the structure. Structural drying is the largest part of it.
