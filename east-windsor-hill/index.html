Water Damage Restoration in East Windsor Hill, South Windsor, CT

East Windsor Hill is the historic northwest corner of South Windsor, along Main Street and Ferry Lane between the Scantic and Connecticut Rivers. If water is spreading in a home here, call and describe what you see.

(844) 950-6900

Where East Windsor Hill sits

The National Register of Historic Places lists the East Windsor Hill Historic District, added in 1986, at about 535 acres in the northwest corner of South Windsor. The listing describes it as roughly bounded by the Scantic River, John Fitch Boulevard, Sullivan Avenue and the Connecticut River.

Main Street runs through it from the Scantic River south to Edwards Cemetery, and the district reaches west to the Connecticut River along Ferry Lane. John Bissell started a ferry across the river to Windsor here in 1648, with its eastern landing at Ferry Lane. The East Windsor Hill Post Office at 1865 Main Street has operated since 1837.

Those are historic district limits, not an official neighborhood boundary, so use them as a guide.

Historic homes and how drying is handled

The National Register describes the district as mostly residential, with well-preserved buildings from about 1700 to 1860 in Georgian, Federal and Greek Revival styles. Many properties keep barns and outbuildings, and at least one is still a working farm.

Age alone does not tell you whether a house will have water damage. It does change the repair. Original trim, wide floorboards and carved door surrounds are harder to replace than new materials, so ask the crew how they plan to dry them in place and what readings will justify removing anything.

The Historic District Commission oversees the local historic district on Main Street and Ferry Lane. Before assuming what exterior repairs need approval, ask the commission or the Town.

Rivers, flood maps and what is not verified

Two rivers form edges of the district. The Capitol Region hazard plan lists the Connecticut, Scantic and Hockanum Rivers as South Windsor's main watercourses. It also says the Town does not allow new construction in the FEMA floodplain and that some homes and buildings are flood prone.

The plan does not name East Windsor Hill or Ferry Lane as flood-prone. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA flood map for your own address before drawing any conclusion.

Flood damage is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy. The Connecticut Insurance Department says it needs a separate flood policy, with a 30-day waiting period.

First steps while you wait for help

  1. Keep people and pets out of any room where water reaches outlets, cords or the panel.
  2. Close the main water valve if a pipe or appliance is the source and the valve is dry and reachable.
  3. Do not run an ordinary vacuum on standing water.
  4. Lift small valuables and rugs out of the wet area.
  5. Photograph the damage and the source when it is safe.
  6. Call us and describe where the water came from and how far it reached.

How extraction and drying work

The crew removes standing water first, then reads moisture in floors, walls and framing with a meter. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the readings match a dry reference. Damp materials left in place can support mold growth, so speed matters. Contaminated water from a sewer or river is a different job, with protective gear and removal of porous materials.

Insurance paperwork

Before cleanup, the Connecticut Insurance Department advises you to photograph and video the damage and keep every receipt. Your policy and the cause of the damage decide what is covered, so ask your insurer. Moisture readings and dated photos can help describe the work. They do not guarantee a claim will be paid.

East Windsor Hill questions

Is East Windsor Hill in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified. The district borders two rivers, but a nearby river does not put every property in a mapped zone. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer.

Can water damage in an older house be dried without tearing it apart?

Often some of it can. The crew should show moisture readings and explain what must come out and what can be dried in place. Ask before anything original is removed.

Would my policy pay for a pipe that burst in an old house?

Many policies cover sudden and accidental pipe failure, and many exclude flood and slow leaks. Coverage depends on your policy and the cause, so ask your insurer.

Do local historic district rules affect repairs?

They may affect exterior work along Main Street and Ferry Lane. Ask the Historic District Commission or the Town before you plan changes to the outside of a building.
